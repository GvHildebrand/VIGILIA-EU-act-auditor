# Rules — how this auditor works

The procedure is fixed. Run it in this order, every time. Skipping step 1 or 2 is
how audits produce confident findings about obligations the artifact never owed.

---

## Step 1 — Scope the artifact

Read the evidence pack. Fill in this table from **stated facts only**. Every row
gets a source. A row the pack does not answer is written "not stated", and every
obligation that turns on it is ruled `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`.

| Question | Why it decides something |
|---|---|
| Provider, deployer, or both? | 50(1) and 50(2) bind **providers**; 50(3) and 50(4) bind **deployers**. Getting this wrong is the most common way to produce a false CRITICAL. Article 3(11) makes putting a system into service *for own use* enough to be a provider. |
| Which output modalities are generated? | 50(2) covers audio, image, video **and text**. 50(4) first subparagraph is image, audio, video only. |
| Available to persons in the EU? | Decides whether the Regulation reaches the artifact at all. |
| Date placed on the market / put into service | Decides whether **Article 111(4)** shelters the 50(2) duty until 2 December 2026. |
| Emotion recognition or biometric categorisation present? | The 50(3) trigger, and it is a definitional question — Article 3(39) and 3(40), not the operator's internal vocabulary. |
| Is generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest? | The 50(4) second-subparagraph trigger. Both limbs required. |
| Law-enforcement authorisation claimed? | An exemption in 50(1), 50(2), 50(3) and 50(4). |
| SME / SMC? | Reverses the direction of the penalty cap — Article 99(6) and 99(6a). |

The definitions that settle these are in
[`reference/32024R1689/article-3-definitions.md`](reference/32024R1689/article-3-definitions.md).
Use them. An operator calling something "emotion detection" does not make it an
emotion recognition system within Article 3(39), and the reverse mistake is just
as common.

---

## Step 2 — Applicability, one obligation at a time

For each of the eleven obligations in
[`provisions/article-50.md`](provisions/article-50.md):

**a. Is the trigger met?** Against the scoping table, not against impressions.
Trigger not met → `NOT_APPLICABLE`, `basis: trigger_not_met`, with the reason.

**b. Test every exemption in the provision's own words.** Each exemption has an id
and a verbatim quote in the register. Name the ones you tested and rejected, not
only the one you applied — a reader needs to see that the escape hatches were
considered. An exemption that is satisfied → `NOT_APPLICABLE` with **that
exemption's id** as the basis, never `PASS`. The distinction is not pedantry:
`PASS` asserts a duty was discharged, and an operator relying on an exemption has
discharged nothing and will lose the position if the exemption stops applying.

Watch **EUAIA-50-4-X2**, the artistic and satirical carve-out. It does not remove
the duty; it reduces it to disclosing the existence of generated content in a way
that does not hamper enjoyment of the work. Treating it as a full exemption is an
error.

**c. Apply the dates.**

| Rule | Effect |
|---|---|
| Article 113 | Article 50 applies from **2 August 2026**. It is in force. |
| **Article 111(4)** | A system generating synthetic content **placed on the market before 2 August 2026** owes Article 50(2) by **2 December 2026**. Set `applicability: transitional` and name the deadline in the finding. |

Article 111(4) was inserted by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 and is **not in the 2024
Official Journal text**. It is reproduced at
[`reference/32026R1744/amendments-to-article-50-and-111.md`](reference/32026R1744/amendments-to-article-50-and-111.md).
An auditor working from the 2024 text alone gets this wrong.

---

## Step 3 — Evidence

For every obligation that applies, locate the evidence in the artifact and quote
it with a locator: `path:L12`, a URL with a selector, or a screenshot region.

Absent or insufficient evidence is not a FAIL. It is `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`, with
an `evidence_needed` field saying exactly what would resolve it. The register lists
the evidence each obligation requires.

The recurring case is Article 50(2). Whether output is "marked in a machine-readable
format" **cannot be determined by looking at a screen**. It needs a metadata dump —
a C2PA manifest, an IPTC `digitalSourceType`, a watermark detector result, a
response header. Without one, the honest verdict is `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`. A
visible "Generated by AI" badge is evidence about 50(4) and 50(5); it is not
evidence about 50(2).

---

## Step 3b — Record how you know

Every finding carries a `provenance` field. It is not decoration: it is the answer
to "what if the operator lied to you".

| Value | Means |
|---|---|
| `observed` | you saw the artifact itself — a rendered surface, a real output file, a response header |
| `inferred` | you derived it from something *about* the artifact — source code, an archive snapshot, a public register |
| `declared` | the operator said so, and nothing independent confirms it |
| `none` | the provision imposes no duty, so there is nothing to evidence. Only for `NOTED`. |

Prefer `observed`. Where a fact can be checked against the artifact rather than
taken from the questionnaire, check it — the operator is usually the *weakest*
available source for anything Article 50 asks about, because almost all of it is
about what reaches a person and that is visible.

Every report totals these in its header and states plainly how many verdicts would
collapse if the operator's statements were false.
`_verify/verify_citations.py` recomputes the totals and fails the report if the
header understates them. **An audit may not hide how much it is trusting.**

---

## Step 4 — Verdict

| Verdict | When |
|---|---|
| `PASS` | The obligation applies and the evidence shows it is met. |
| `FAIL` | The obligation applies and the evidence shows it is not met. |
| `PARTIAL` | The obligation applies and is met on some surfaces or in some respects but not others. Say which. |
| `NOT_APPLICABLE` | The trigger is not met, or a named exemption is satisfied. Requires `basis`. |
| `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` | Cannot be determined from the pack. Requires `evidence_needed`. |
| `NOTED` | The provision imposes no duty on the audited party — 50(6) and 50(7) only. |

Every obligation gets exactly one. Eleven findings, always.

---

## Step 5 — Severity, computed

Severity is not a judgement about how bad something feels. It is a function of
three inputs, each of which comes from the standard.

**Input 1 — penalty tier.** Article 99(4)(g) puts *every* Article 50 infringement
in the same tier: up to EUR 15 000 000 or 3 % of total worldwide annual turnover,
whichever is higher (lower, for SMEs and SMCs, under 99(6) and 99(6a)). Because it
is constant across Article 50, it does not discriminate between findings. It sets
the stakes; it does not set the severity.

**Input 2 — duty force**, from the provision's own verb. `absolute` (an unqualified
"shall"), `qualified` ("as far as this is technically feasible"), or
`no_direct_duty` (50(6), 50(7)).

**Input 3 — applicability today.** `in_force`, `transitional`, `not_yet_applicable`.

### The matrix

| | |
|---|---|
| verdict `PASS` or `NOT_APPLICABLE` | **NONE** |
| verdict `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` | **UNRESOLVED** |
| verdict `NOTED`, or duty force `no_direct_duty` | **OBSERVATION** |
| applicability `not_yet_applicable` | **OBSERVATION** |
| applicability `transitional` | **MINOR** |
| duty force `qualified` | **MINOR** |
| `absolute` + `in_force` + `FAIL` | **CRITICAL** |
| `absolute` + `in_force` + `PARTIAL` | **MAJOR** |

First matching row wins, read top to bottom. That is the whole rule, and
`_verify/verify_citations.py` recomputes it for every finding and fails the report if
the declared severity differs.

Two consequences are deliberate and neither is negotiable:

- **A qualified duty can never be CRITICAL.** The provision qualified itself.
- **A defect inside a transitional window is MINOR, however grave it looks.** The
  Regulation gave the operator until 2 December 2026. When that date passes the
  same evidence returns CRITICAL, with no change to the auditor. Say so in the
  finding, with the date.

---

## Step 6 — Citation form

Fixed, and machine-checked:

```
provision id  →  verbatim quote  →  reference/<file>:L<line>
```

Three rules:

1. **The quote is the Regulation's words**, byte for byte, from the cited line.
2. **Cite the authentic text.** `reference/32024R1689/` and
   `reference/32026R1744/` are the Official Journal. The consolidated text under
   `reference/02024R1689-20260727/` is a documentation tool with no legal effect
   and citing it as authority is rejected by the verifier.
3. **Evidence gets a locator too.** A finding says where in the artifact it looked.

---

## Step 7 — Assemble

Use [`_templates/audit-report.md`](_templates/audit-report.md). Prose carries the
reasoning; the fenced `finding` blocks carry the machine-checkable determination.

The report ends with an attestation naming the reference fingerprint and register
version it was written against, so a reader can tell whether the standard has moved
since. Then run:

```bash
python3 _verify/verify_citations.py <report>
```

**A report that does not pass is not finished.** The script does not check whether a
verdict is legally right — nothing can. It checks that no provision was invented,
no quote was fabricated, no obligation was skipped, and no severity was hand-waved.
That is the floor, not the ceiling.

## Standing cautions

**"Article 52" does not exist.** It was the draft numbering. The adopted Article 52
concerns the classification procedure for general-purpose AI models, which is a
different subject entirely.

**Article 50 is not Article 53.** Article 50 binds providers and deployers of
certain AI *systems*. Training-data summaries, technical documentation for
downstream providers, copyright policy and systemic-risk obligations belong to
Articles 53 and 55, on providers of general-purpose AI *models*. Conflating them is
the single most common error in published commentary on this Article — including
in commentary written by people who audit it for a living. Fixture 03 in this
repository documents a live instance.

**Article 50 applies irrespective of risk tier.** It is not the "limited risk"
article. Recital 132 says the transparency obligations apply "irrespective of
whether they qualify as high-risk or not". Compliance tooling that gates Article 50
behind a limited-risk classification is wrong.

**The Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content is voluntary.** The
Commission assessed it adequate on 8 July 2026, and the AI Board on 9 July 2026,
for Articles 50(2), (4) and (5). It creates no obligations and confers no
presumption of conformity. Adherence is evidence toward a finding; it is never a
verdict. See [`reference/code-of-practice/`](reference/code-of-practice/).
